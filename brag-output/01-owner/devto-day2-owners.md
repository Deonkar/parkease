Somewhere in Bangalore right now there's a driveway with nothing in it. A basement slot that sits empty from nine to six. A space someone pays for and never uses.

And three streets away, a driver is circling the same block for the fourth time.

**ParkEase** puts those two people together. Yesterday I showed you the ledger underneath. Today, the part that matters: what it's like to be the owner.

## Five things you can do (the whole video is under thirty seconds)

**1. List your space in five quick steps.**
Where it is, what it's called, how many cars and bikes fit, your price, when it's available. Submit, and your listing waits for approval before it goes live.

**2. Set your own price.**
Per hour, per slot, separately for cars and for two-wheelers. Your spot, your number.

**3. Check drivers in with one scan.**
When a driver arrives they show a QR code. You scan it, and your phone confirms who it is, their vehicle number and their booked time. No paper, no guessing, no "I booked, I promise."

**4. See every booking, line by line.**
Every booking shows three lines: the price, the ParkEase fee, and exactly what you earned. No mystery deductions. (Yesterday's post is the story of why that line is harder to get right than it looks.)

**5. Watch it add up.**
Today's earnings, this week's, with a chart that fills in as the week goes.

That's the whole owner app today. It's small on purpose.

## What I'm not telling you yet

Everything above happens on the owner's phone. The other half of ParkEase is a driver looking at a map of free spots nearby.

For a while, on the web build, that map was empty. No error on screen. Every test green. Just a blank box where Bangalore should be. And when the map finally came back, every parking spot on it was piled into the top-left corner.
